A data connector copies data from a **source** to a **sink** without your app doing the copying. There are two ways it reads. **Change data capture** (CDC) reads a database's own change log, so every committed insert, update and delete flows out in order, and the app writes only once. **Polling** asks a SaaS API, such as a CRM or a payments provider, for new rows every few minutes.

The sink is always a little behind the source. With change capture the lag is small: the time to read the log and write the change. With polling, a change waits up to a whole poll interval before it starts to move, and the API's rate limit caps how fast a backlog drains. The simulator shows this lag as completion time, and runs the connector's tasks in parallel.

Connectors replace **dual writes**, where the app writes to the database and then to a search index or cache itself. When the second write fails, the two stores disagree and nothing notices. With a connector, the database is the source of truth and the other stores follow it.
